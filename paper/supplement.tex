\section*{S5. Secondary method analyses}
The four analyses below come from archived runs whose per-image predictions were not kept; their numbers are point
estimates without intervals.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Patch-token suppression (SLAS).} A linear probe on DINOv2 patch tokens localises real hair from 5
annotated images (patch AUROC 0.93; 0.97 with 50), but dropping flagged tokens improves reversed AUROC only
modestly ($+0.02$ hair, $+0.08$ thyroid over ROI pooling).
\item \emph{Annotation-free pseudo-groups.} An overlay detector trained on synthetic insertion pairs recognises real
calipers (AUROC 0.84) but not hair (0.57); pseudo-group balancing adds $+0.04$ on thyroid only.
\item \emph{Erasure combined with DFR.} Adds $\le0.014$ over masking plus DFR.
\item \emph{Split-validation selector.} Admitting DFR as a candidate (trained and scored on disjoint validation
halves) fixes the chest-drain case (0.656 vs 0.538) but makes choices noisier elsewhere; neither selector is
uniformly best.
\end{itemize}

\section*{S6. Leakage}
Holding cohort, features and protocol fixed, replacing the grouped split with random image-level splits (93
near-duplicate pairs across train/test) inflates the measured shortcut gap by 18\% (0\% overlap) and 10\% (100\%)
and distorts both masking effects ($+0.184\rightarrow+0.223$; $-0.129\rightarrow-0.088$).

\emph{Paste-edge control.} Neutral tissue from the donor image (or, where no artifact-free window exists, from an

\input{tables/review3_paste_edge}
\emph{Transplant design.} Recipients: artifact-free images of each cohort. Donors: artifact-bearing images of the same
cohort; an artifact instance is the largest connected component of its mask after a grouping dilation (calipers 9\,px,
hair and debris 5\,px), with at least 30 pixels and a bounding-box side of at most 259\,px. For every recipient, donors
are tried in a stable random order (up to 50 donors $\times$ 8 dihedral transforms) until one fits both inside
(overlap $\ge0.95$) and outside (overlap 0) the ROI within the field of view; the instance is alpha-composited with a
Gaussian-blurred mask. Environments as in the controlled sweeps (0.9/0.1 training and correlated test, 0.1/0.9 reversed
test, clean test without artifact), 5-fold grouped cross-validation, five presence seeds (bootstrap clusters).
Recipients without a feasible donor (mostly small capsule lesion boxes) are excluded; counts by label are in
\texttt{results/review2/transplant/*/feasibility\_by\_label.csv}.

\section*{S15. Per-seed versus crossed bootstrap}
The original estimator resamples training-seed clusters and then test images independently within each sampled cluster.
When the clusters share test images---every seed of an analysis scores the same official thyroid test split, the same
transplant recipients, and largely the same trap pool---this treats one test set as several and understates the
test-set sampling variance. The crossed estimator resamples clusters and draws one Poisson weight per test image, shared
by every cluster (\texttt{wtss.stats.crossed\_auc\_bootstrap}). The table gives the crossed CI of each headline claim,
its width relative to the per-seed CI of the same claim and whether the ``excludes zero'' verdict changed; the per-seed
CIs themselves, and the same comparison for all 2,466 intervals of the regenerated runs (median width ratio 1.56, IQR
1.34--1.74; 179 verdicts changed) and for the 263 intervals of the four analyses regenerated later (LaMa, template
erasure, chest drains, chest devices; 32 verdicts changed), are listed in \texttt{results/bootstrap\_correction/}. None of the four changed
verdicts below carries a claim of the paper. Every interval in the paper and this supplement is a crossed one; the
exceptions (image bootstrap for label agreement, point estimates for runs without per-image predictions) are listed in
the protocol section of the main text. Generated by \texttt{scripts/analysis/crossed\_ci.py}.

\paragraph{Masking implementations and the real overlap} Five views per image from the cached 518-px image and its

\section*{S17. Is there a remedy that dominates masking?}
Two registrations (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION\_ROUND8} and \texttt{ROUND9}; registry in S1) and a documented search between
them (\texttt{docs/ROUND9\_SEARCH\_LEDGER.md}). Candidates are heads on frozen DINOv2-B/14 features of the masked image
(or the full image); every setting is chosen per training set on validation data by the worst-group validation AUROC,
subject to the same-artifact validation AUROC staying within 0.005 of masking's, with masking as fallback. A candidate
dominates if every component of the criterion holds (intersection--union test, one-sided 0.025 each); across candidates a
fixed sequence registered in advance. Confirmation: trap resamples generated only after each registration (seeds
8101--8505 and 9101--9505), ovary held out from every choice; the unaltered test sets and ISIC 2020 had been seen before
and are semi-confirmatory. Full reports: \texttt{stages/stage9\_remedies\_dominance}.

\section*{S13. Reproducibility}
