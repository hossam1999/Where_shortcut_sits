\section*{S1. Pre-registration index and registry}
Every experiment was specified in a dated document committed to the public repository before any model was fitted on
the corresponding data; amendments were committed before the amended analysis. The registry lists, for every
registration, its first commit and the first commit that added outcome results (outcome-free cell counts excluded;
for registrations that add arms to an existing results folder, the first commit whose results contain the new arm).
Commit times are set by the committing machine and are therefore not an external time stamp; the registrations of the
second review round (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION\_REVIEW2}) were additionally pushed to GitHub before any model was fitted.
The primary family used for multiplicity control (\texttt{STATISTICAL\_PLAN}) was assembled after the individual
results were available; each member test was individually pre-registered, and the Holm correction is applied to the
whole family. Generated by \texttt{scripts/verify/registry\_table.py}.

\section*{S2. Replication of the original pilot}
Of 88 numbers reported in the thesis proposal, 73 are reproduced exactly (same sign, same confidence-interval
verdict, $|\Delta|\le0.02$), 12 agree in sign and significance with a larger difference, and 3 differ (a borderline
occlusion interval, a contrast trap that was reported as null and is not, and one source stratum). Details:
\texttt{results/SUMMARY.md}. The archived pilot confidence intervals are recomputed exactly from the archived
predictions (\texttt{make verify}).

\section*{S3. Datasets considered and not used}
CANDID-PTX (not accessible outside Health New Zealand); RANZCR-CLiP devices linked to NIH (masking helped at both
locations; device placement confounded with other devices); chest-radiograph cardiomegaly with lines over the heart
(210 labelled images); TCGA/GrandQC pathology (pen-mark masks unreliable); ISIC ink (70 in-lesion images);
breast ultrasound BUSI/BUS-BRA (caliper-free group only $\approx70\%$ pure after a two-detector design); DDTI
thyroid (TI-RADS instead of pathology; 134 images); BKAI-IGH colonoscopy specular highlights (no highlight-free
images). \emph{External validation with patient identifiers.} TN-SCUI 2020 (one image per patient, nodule masks)
may not be used outside the challenge under its terms. ThyUS2Path \citep{hou2024thyus2path} (842 patients, 8,508
images, surgical pathology, CC BY 4.0) contains real calipers in several colours but no nodule masks, and our frozen
caliper detector responds to elements of its scanner interface (frame lines, depth scales) on a random 25\% sample
inspected before any label was read. Using it requires a nodule segmentation transferred from TN3K and a caliper
detector re-validated on this interface; we did not run it.

\section*{S4. Artifact labels: audits, sensitivity and an expert audit kit}
Visual audit of 40 random images per cell by the analysis team (not clinicians): thyroid detections 95\% on real
calipers; caliper-free thyroid images 95--97\% without visible calipers; ovary 40/40 detections are overlays, 31/40
calipers; caliper-free ovary images 92\% without calipers but $\approx$28\% with other overlays. Capsule debris probe:
pixel accuracy 0.92, IoU 0.62 against expert masks. Re-running with large-marker ($\ge50$ px) and strict-location
($r\ge0.7$ / $r<0.05$) definitions (thyroid, ovary) and a strict capsule Trap~B leaves every primary conclusion
unchanged (\texttt{SENSITIVITY\_ARTIFACT\_LABELS}). \emph{Expert audit (prepared, not yet carried out).}
\texttt{scripts/audit/make\_expert\_audit\_kit.py} draws 60 images per cohort (half flagged by the automatic label),
renders each blind (ROI contour only) and with the automatic mask, and writes a rating sheet (artifact present;
location relative to the ROI; mask quality); \texttt{scripts/audit/score\_expert\_audit.py} computes Cohen's
$\kappa$ between two independent raters and the precision and recall of the automatic labels against their
consensus, with bootstrap CIs.

\section*{S5. Secondary method analyses}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Patch-token suppression (SLAS).} A linear probe on DINOv2 patch tokens localises real hair from 5
annotated images (patch AUROC 0.93; 0.97 with 50), but dropping flagged tokens improves reversed AUROC only
modestly ($+0.02$ hair, $+0.08$ thyroid over ROI pooling).
\item \emph{Annotation-free pseudo-groups.} An overlay detector trained on synthetic insertion pairs recognises real
calipers (AUROC 0.84) but not hair (0.57); pseudo-group balancing adds $+0.04$ on thyroid only.
\item \emph{Erasure combined with DFR.} Adds $\le0.014$ over masking plus DFR.
\item \emph{Split-validation selector.} Admitting DFR as a candidate (trained and scored on disjoint validation
halves) fixes the chest-drain case (0.656 vs 0.538) but makes choices noisier elsewhere; neither selector is
uniformly best.
\end{itemize}

\section*{S6. Leakage}
Holding cohort, features and protocol fixed, replacing the grouped split with random image-level splits (93
near-duplicate pairs across train/test) inflates the measured shortcut gap by 18\% (0\% overlap) and 10\% (100\%)
and distorts both masking effects ($+0.184\rightarrow+0.223$; $-0.129\rightarrow-0.088$).

\section*{S10. Absolute performance and published results}
Only the thyroid cohort has a published benchmark on the same split and labels (official patient-disjoint TNCD split,
614 test images). The ovarian (benign cystic versus solid-component tumours) and capsule (erosion versus polyp-like)
binary tasks and the ISIC 2019 leave-one-hospital-out melanoma task were defined for this study and have no published
counterpart; their clean AUROC is reported with every result table. Frozen encoders with a linear head are below
fine-tuned CNNs; the location law replicates with fine-tuned networks (main text).

\emph{Transplant design.} Recipients: artifact-free images of each cohort. Donors: artifact-bearing images of the same
cohort; an artifact instance is the largest connected component of its mask after a grouping dilation (calipers 9\,px,
hair and debris 5\,px), with at least 30 pixels and a bounding-box side of at most 259\,px. For every recipient, donors
are tried in a stable random order (up to 50 donors $\times$ 8 dihedral transforms) until one fits both inside
(overlap $\ge0.95$) and outside (overlap 0) the ROI within the field of view; the instance is alpha-composited with a
Gaussian-blurred mask. Environments as in the controlled sweeps (0.9/0.1 training and correlated test, 0.1/0.9 reversed
test, clean test without artifact), 5-fold grouped cross-validation, five presence seeds (bootstrap clusters).
Recipients without a feasible donor (mostly small capsule lesion boxes) are excluded; counts by label are in
\texttt{results/review2/transplant/*/feasibility\_by\_label.csv}.

\section*{S13. Reproducibility}
All numbers in the main paper and this supplement are produced by scripts in the repository (\texttt{scripts/});
tables are generated, never typed, and every confidence interval in the paper is traced automatically to a result
file (\texttt{scripts/verify/audit\_paper\_numbers.py}). A complete rebuild on different hardware reproduces every
primary crossover within 0.01 (main text). Compute: one 24\,GB GPU.
