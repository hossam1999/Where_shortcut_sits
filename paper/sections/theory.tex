\section{Why location matters: a linear-Gaussian account}\label{sec:theory}
Let a frozen encoder yield a disease block with class-separation signal-to-noise ratio $S$ and an artifact block with
SNR $A$ per unit of artifact presence; training uses $P(A{=}1\mid Y{=}1)=p_1$, $P(A{=}1\mid Y{=}0)=p_0$ and the
reversed test swaps them. For the LDA-optimal linear head the artifact's effective training SNR is
$\tilde A=(p_1-p_0)^2A/(1+p_1(1-p_1)A)$ and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUROC}_{\mathrm{rev}}(S,\tilde A)=\Phi\!\left(\frac{S-\tilde A}{\sqrt{2(S+\tilde A)}}\right),
\end{equation}
increasing in $S$ and decreasing in $\tilde A$ (verified by simulation to within 0.009 AUROC; Supplement~S14).
Masking maps $(S,\tilde A)\mapsto(S_m,\tilde A_m)$: out of the ROI $\tilde A_m=0$; inside, $\tilde A_m\ge\tilde A$.
Hence (i) in-ROI masking harms when the mask removes disease-informative context ($S_m<S$), and can harm even when it
does not if the artifact becomes a larger share of what remains ($\tilde A_m>\tilde A$); (ii) for any $S_m$ the benefit of masking is larger out of the ROI
than inside it---the crossover is always positive; (iii) erasing a subspace that contains the artifact direction sets
$\tilde A\to0$ but scales $S$ by $1-\rho^2$, where $\rho$ is its overlap with the disease direction, so erasure fails
when the artifact resembles the pathology unless the disease direction is protected; (iv) group balancing removes the artifact--label association and everything
correlated with it, whatever the location; (v) a shortcut carried by correlates that neither the mask nor pixel-level
erasure touch can only be removed by label-based reweighting. Each consequence is borne out in
Sections~\ref{sec:law}--\ref{sec:guide}.

\paragraph{Does the account match real encoders?} The same model gives the AUROC for any test composition, so $(S,A)$
can be fitted to each trained model's clean and correlated AUROC \emph{only} and the reversed-test AUROC predicted from
them; no parameter is shared across models or tuned. Over 136 ERM and masking models (real traps and controlled sweeps)
the held-out reversed-test AUROC is predicted with a mean absolute error of 0.052 ($r=0.91$), against 0.129 for a
linear-symmetry heuristic (reversed $=2\cdot$clean$-$correlated) and 0.237 for ``reversed $=$ clean''
(Fig.~\ref{fig:theory_predict}). The account also anticipates DermLIP's missing hair gap. For the crossover itself the
heuristic does almost as well, so we do not use the crossover correlation as evidence. Adding the chest-radiograph
cells (154 models) leaves the error almost unchanged (0.053), but one near-zero device crossover has the wrong sign,
which fails our pre-registered all-signs criterion; the account is therefore a qualitative one, and it fails, as
consequence (v) says it must, for chest drains, where the shortcut is carried by the treated lung (implied reversed
AUROC 0.72--0.78, observed 0.19--0.23).

\paragraph{Prospective test (pre-registered)} The comparison above uses results that existed when the test was
designed. We therefore registered the same procedure, unchanged, before the masking-implementation and overlap-bin
analyses were run. On their 84 cells the account predicted the held-out reversed AUROC with a mean absolute error of
0.011 ($r=0.996$), against 0.036 for the symmetry heuristic and 0.177 for ``reversed $=$ clean'', and met its registered
criterion for quantitative prediction within frozen-encoder linear heads on the four real-artifact cohorts.
