\paragraph{What the evidence supports} Masking is not a neutral preprocessing step: its benefit depends on where the
artifact sits. Four designs agree---real traps, covariate-matched traps, the transplant on fixed images and controlled
sweeps---and the effect holds for every masking implementation we tried, falls continuously with the real overlap,
replicates on new patients and grows when images with contradicted labels are removed. The claim is about the
\emph{gap}: whether in-ROI masking helps a little or harms depends on whether the mask also removes disease context,
which the linear-Gaussian account makes explicit and which differs between modalities. The transplant's paste-edge
control shows that what matters is the location of \emph{any} label-correlated pattern, not a particular artifact's
appearance, so the problem cannot be solved by recognising particular artifacts; the evidence that real artifacts behave
this way in natural images comes from the real and matched traps and the unaltered test data.

\paragraph{Why this is not obvious} That a mask cannot remove what it keeps sounds self-evident. Three findings go
beyond it. Masking makes the model rely on an in-ROI artifact \emph{more}, about twice as much in the ruler sweep,
because it removes the competing evidence. The consequence is invisible in overall AUROC yet clinical: on the
patient-disjoint thyroid split a fine-tuned model near the published benchmark misses more of the malignant nodules
that carry a caliper, and part of that loss survives re-tuning the threshold on the test set itself. And the effect is
predictable: fitted only to clean and correlated performance, a two-parameter account predicted 84 registered results
before they existed. An ROI-masked model can therefore look unchanged, even after local recalibration, while failing on
exactly the cases in which the shortcut points the wrong way; audits of masked models should stratify performance by
artifact presence and location, and report reversed, correlated and clean performance together, because a
reversed-environment gain can also be manufactured by removing diagnosis signal.

\paragraph{Limitations}
(i) \emph{Artifact labels.} Calipers and capsule debris are segmented automatically and hair masks are published
semi-automatic masks; no clinician audited them. Independent sources confirm where hair lies but show that our
hair-presence threshold over-calls hair; the thyroid caliper labels agree with two independent labellers where those
agree with each other, but the registered cleaning rule could not be tested, and the ovary and capsule labels remain
unchecked. A blinded rating by the first author, a non-clinician, is registered and incomplete. Removing contradicted
images or using stricter definitions strengthens rather than weakens the results, and the transplant does not depend on
the labels because the pasted instance is identical at both locations.
(ii) \emph{Patient identifiers and external validation.} TN3K/TNCD, MMOTU and SEE-AI publish no patient identifiers for
the pooled images, so trap splits group near-duplicates by perceptual hash (the official thyroid test split is
patient-disjoint). External validation on new patients was possible for dermoscopy only (Supplement~S3).
(iii) \emph{Constructed shifts.} Reversed environments are constructed by subsampling; real shifts are less extreme,
which is why we report the unaltered test data separately.
(iv) \emph{Frozen encoders.} Most analyses use frozen encoders with linear heads, below the best published fine-tuned
models on the one cohort with a comparable benchmark (0.735 versus 0.773--0.799 AUROC). A fine-tuned model near that
benchmark shows every registered harm, and the law replicates with fine-tuning in the three cohorts where the network
learned the task; the fine-tuned ovary network did not.
(v) \emph{Scope.} Chest radiography is a boundary case, and the linear-Gaussian account cannot anticipate a shortcut
carried by correlates that the clean and correlated environments do not expose.
(vi) \emph{Registration and statistics.} Commit times are set by the committing machine, so only registrations pushed to
GitHub before their runs carry an external time stamp; the 16-test primary family was assembled after the individual
results, and two sensitivity analyses were registered after the result they examine. Matching left within-label
standardised differences of up to 0.19 for some covariates.
(vii) \emph{Thresholds.} Most of the thyroid sensitivity loss at validation-fixed thresholds is threshold transport; the
part that survives re-tuning has a wide interval.
(viii) \emph{Remedies.} No remedy dominates masking; the best candidates fail on one cell, ISIC 2020 overall, by
0.01--0.02 AUROC, and the dominance tests use frozen DINOv2 features.
