\section{What to use instead of masking}\label{sec:guide}\label{sec:solution}
\paragraph{A class-conditional constraint on the masked features} Let $x$ be the frozen features of the masked image,
$y$ the diagnosis and $a$ the image-level artifact label from the automatic detectors of Sec.~\ref{sec:data}, used during
training only. On the training images we compute the within-class mean differences
$\delta_y=\mu(x\mid a{=}1,y)-\mu(x\mid a{=}0,y)$, project the features onto the orthogonal complement of $\delta_0,\delta_1$
(or of their average; chosen on validation data) and fit an ordinary logistic head on all training images at their
natural weights. Within each class, the head then cannot score images with and without the artifact differently on
average, and no image is down-weighted; group balancing, by contrast, loses on unaltered data mainly by discarding
disease evidence through reweighting (Supplement~S17). The constraint is the first-moment, linear case of
counterfactual invariance \citep{veitch2021counterfactual} and of the auxiliary-label conditional-independence
regularisers of \citet{makar2022causally}; what is new is its derivation from the location law and its use after
masking. At test time it needs only the masked image.

\paragraph{Registered test against masking} We registered a criterion for \emph{dominating} masking: not worse (margin
0.01 AUROC) on the six unaltered test sets and on the Trap~B and clean tests, better on every Trap~A and on the
conflicting pairs of the unaltered test sets, and no shortcut flipping---42 components, all of which must hold
(Supplement~S17). Settings were chosen on validation data only; confirmation used trap resamples generated after the
registration and one cohort (ovary) that never influenced a choice. Table~\ref{tab:remedies} compares every remedy with
masking on these data. Group balancing, alone or after U-MtE, gains the most where masking fails, but pays where masking
already works: it is worse than masking on the unaltered test sets overall in three of six (e.g.\ ISIC 2020 $-0.025$ and
$-0.035$), because reweighting discards disease evidence. The constraint keeps most of that gain---better than masking in
every Trap~A, by $+0.118$ (held-out ovary) to $+0.330$ (thyroid, from 0.400 to 0.730), and on every set of conflicting
pairs---without the cost: it is never worse on a Trap~B test and is within the registered margin on four of the six unaltered test sets. It
met 40 of 42 components (Fig.~\ref{fig:dom}); the two it failed are the thyroid split, where non-inferiority is
inconclusive ($-0.002$ [$-0.011, +0.007$]), and ISIC 2020 overall ($-0.021$ [$-0.027, -0.016$]), where in-lesion hair
still points to melanoma for most patients.

\paragraph{Nothing does better} A documented search over twelve further ideas on development data only---among them
backdoor adjustment, product of experts, logit adjustment, moment matching, conditional adversarial debiasing,
contrastive and invariance methods---and a second registered round of its three best on new data gave the same answer:
IRMv1 met 41 of 42 components and lost only on ISIC 2020 overall ($-0.009$), and the constraint, refitted with its
frozen recipe, replicated its result (loss on ISIC 2020 only, $-0.015$; Supplement~S17). Every alternative
to masking, including the unmasked model, loses overall on ISIC 2020, mostly on pairs whose images share the artifact
status. No remedy we tested dominates masking.

\paragraph{Without artifact labels} Erasing from the masked features the subspace along which \emph{generic}
synthetic overlays move them (mask-then-erase, U-MtE; Supplement~S8) removes much of what masking leaves for
overlay-like artifacts (thyroid calipers in Trap~A: from 0.400 to 0.624) at almost no cost on the unaltered test sets,
but it is worse than masking for debris that resembles the pathology (capsule: 0.548 versus 0.667) and gains little where
the shortcut is carried by correlates of hair (Table~\ref{tab:remedies}).

\paragraph{Decision guide} (i) Measure where artifacts sit relative to the ROI; for artifacts outside it, masking is
enough. (ii) For artifacts inside it, with image-level artifact labels from an automatic detector, use the
class-conditional constraint on the masked features, and expect a small overall cost where the deployment data still
reward the shortcut; balancing gains more on the conflicting cases but costs more on the unaltered distribution. (iii) Without artifact labels, U-MtE helps for overlay-like artifacts; correlate-carried shortcuts
need labels. (iv) Evaluate any remedy on shortcut-conflicting cases \emph{and} on the unaltered distribution, with clean,
correlated and reversed performance together, because a reversed-environment gain can be manufactured by removing
diagnosis signal.
