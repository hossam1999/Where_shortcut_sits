\section{Consequences on unaltered test data}\label{sec:natural}\label{sec:robust}
The traps construct an extreme reversal. Without any resampling, in-ROI artifacts are still associated with the label
(thyroid calipers: 37.6\% of benign vs 24.1\% of malignant nodules; in-lesion hair: 31\% of melanomas vs 20\% of benign
lesions; capsule debris on the lesion: 23\% of polyp-like lesions vs 10\% of erosions). We trained on natural training
data and tested on untouched data: the official, patient-disjoint TN3K/TNCD test split \citep{gong2022acl}, each ISIC
source held out in turn, and held-out capsule frame blocks. We report AUROC on \emph{shortcut-conflicting} pairs, in
which the artifact points to the wrong diagnosis, beside AUROC over all pairs.

\paragraph{Operating points (pre-registered)} A clinical decision uses a threshold. With thresholds fixed on each
seed's validation split at five operating points (Table~\ref{tab:op}), masking lowers sensitivity on the official
thyroid split at all five, for example from \input{tables/review2_op_inline}. Specificity rises correspondingly, so part
of the change is a shift of the scores relative to the validation thresholds. The loss is largest where the shortcut
conflicts with the label: among the 78 malignant nodules that carry an in-ROI caliper, sensitivity at a validation
specificity of 0.80 falls by \input{tables/review2_op_conflict}. For this frozen model the subgroup was defined after
its AUROC harm was found and only the operating-point test was registered in advance (Supplement~S18).

\paragraph{A fine-tuned model near the benchmark (pre-registered)} To test whether these are properties of a weak
model, we chose a fine-tuning recipe on validation images only (ConvNeXt-T pretrained on ImageNet-22k), registered the
subgroup test before any result of this model existed and trained it with five seeds. Its test AUROC is 0.769, 0.004
below the lowest published cross-entropy baseline on this split (0.773; \citealp{gong2022acl}). All four registered
tests are supported (Holm): masking lowers the AUROC on conflicting pairs ($-0.102$ [$-0.178, -0.026$]), lowers
sensitivity at four of five operating points, lowers it among the 78 conflicting malignant nodules from 0.744 to 0.223
($-0.521$ [$-0.642, -0.382$]), and its trap crossover is $+0.187$ [$+0.111, +0.268$].

\paragraph{Is the loss a threshold artefact? (pre-registered)} Re-tuning each model's threshold on the test set
itself---the best that recalibration at a deployment site can achieve---makes the overall sensitivity of the two arms
equal at a test specificity of 0.80, but the conflicting subgroup still loses: from 0.544 to 0.390 ($-0.154$
[$-0.302, -0.023$]) for the fine-tuned model and from 0.505 to 0.331 ($-0.174$ [$-0.313, -0.012$]) for the frozen probe
(Holm $p=0.019$ each). About 30\% of the loss at validation-fixed thresholds therefore cannot be removed by any
threshold; the rest follows from transporting thresholds from validation to test data, as deployment does.

\paragraph{New patients at natural prevalence (pre-registered)} Trained on all ISIC 2019 images and tested on ISIC 2020
without resampling, masking lowers the AUROC on conflicting pairs ($-0.037$ [$-0.061, -0.012$] under a label-free
duplicate rule registered as a sensitivity analysis; $-0.026$ [$-0.052, -0.000$] under the registered rule, which
proved too loose), while overall AUROC is nearly unchanged ($+0.016$ [$-0.000, +0.033$]); group balancing recovers the
conflicting pairs ($+0.072$ [$+0.048, +0.097$] over masking). No sensitivity loss at a fixed operating point was
detectable (Supplement~S18).
