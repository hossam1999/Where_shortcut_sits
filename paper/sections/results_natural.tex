\section{Consequences on unaltered test data}\label{sec:natural}\label{sec:robust}
The traps construct an extreme reversal. Without any resampling, in-ROI artifacts are still associated with the label
(thyroid calipers: 37.6\% of benign vs 24.1\% of malignant nodules; in-lesion hair: 31\% of melanomas vs 20\% of benign
lesions; capsule debris on the lesion: 23\% of polyp-like lesions vs 10\% of erosions). We trained on natural training
data and tested on untouched data: the official, patient-disjoint TN3K/TNCD test split \citep{gong2022acl}, each ISIC
source held out in turn, and held-out capsule frame blocks. We report AUROC on \emph{shortcut-conflicting} pairs, in
which the artifact points to the wrong diagnosis, beside AUROC over all pairs.

\paragraph{Discrimination} Table~\ref{tab:natural} collects every unaltered-data result. On shortcut-conflicting pairs
masking \emph{lowers} AUROC in three of five test sets (thyroid $-0.095$ [$-0.167, -0.023$] and two of the three
held-out dermoscopy sources), raises it for the third and leaves capsule unchanged. Over all thyroid pairs it changes
AUROC by only $-0.004$: the harm is confined to the cases in which the in-ROI artifact points to the wrong diagnosis,
which is exactly where a shortcut is dangerous.

\paragraph{Operating points (pre-registered)} A clinical decision uses a threshold. With thresholds fixed on each
seed's validation split, masking lowers sensitivity on the official thyroid split at all five operating points
(Supplement~S9). Specificity rises correspondingly, so part of the change is a shift of the scores relative to the
validation thresholds. The loss is largest where the shortcut conflicts with the label: among the 78 malignant
nodules that carry an in-ROI caliper, sensitivity at a validation specificity of 0.80 falls from 0.549 to 0.177. For
this frozen model the subgroup was defined after its AUROC harm was found and only the operating-point test was
registered in advance.

\paragraph{A fine-tuned model near the benchmark (pre-registered)} To test whether these are properties of a weak
model, we chose a fine-tuning recipe on validation images only (ConvNeXt-T pretrained on ImageNet-22k), registered the
subgroup test before any result of this model existed and trained it with five seeds. Its test AUROC is 0.769, 0.004
below the lowest published cross-entropy baseline on this split (0.773; \citealp{gong2022acl}). All four registered
tests are supported (Holm): the loss on conflicting pairs, the loss of sensitivity at four of five operating points,
the loss among the 78 conflicting nodules---from 0.744 to 0.223 ($-0.521$ [$-0.642, -0.382$])---and a positive trap
crossover.

\paragraph{Is the loss a threshold artefact? (pre-registered)} Re-tuning each model's threshold on the test set
itself---the best that recalibration at a deployment site can achieve---makes the overall sensitivity of the two arms
equal, but the conflicting subgroup still loses, for the fine-tuned model ($-0.154$ [$-0.302, -0.023$]) and for the
frozen probe (Holm $p=0.019$ each; Table~\ref{tab:natural}). About 30\% of the loss at validation-fixed thresholds
therefore cannot be removed by any threshold; the rest follows from transporting thresholds from validation to test
data, as deployment does.

\paragraph{New patients at natural prevalence (pre-registered)} Trained on all ISIC 2019 images and tested on ISIC 2020
without resampling, masking lowers the AUROC on conflicting pairs under both duplicate rules, while overall AUROC is
nearly unchanged; the registered rule proved too loose (it removed most look-alike images of other patients), so a
label-free rule was registered as a sensitivity analysis. Group balancing recovers the conflicting pairs, and no
sensitivity loss at a fixed operating point was detectable (Supplement~S18).
