With the artifact outside the ROI masking removes the shortcut entirely---the masked model's reversed, correlated and clean AUROC coincide---whereas with the identical artifact inside the ROI the shortcut survives masking (Table~\ref{tab:transplant}). The location interaction is positive and Holm-significant in 4 of 4 cohorts: dermoscopic hair $+0.253$ [$+0.231, +0.275$], thyroid $+0.277$ [$+0.264, +0.290$], ovary $+0.074$ [$+0.055, +0.092$], capsule $+0.497$ [$+0.455, +0.539$] (Table~\ref{tab:law}). With real artifacts inside the ROI masking is not only less useful but harmful in dermoscopic hair ($-0.075$ [$-0.095, -0.055$]) and capsule ($-0.175$ [$-0.210, -0.141$]). Outside the ROI the masked model does not react to inserting the artifact at all; inside it, it still reacts strongly (same-head $|\Delta p|$, mask versus ERM: dermoscopic hair 0.297 versus 0.340; thyroid 0.271 versus 0.206; ovary 0.116 versus 0.037; capsule 0.551 versus 0.238). Group balancing, which removes the artifact--label association instead of pixels, is far less location-dependent (interaction -0.052 to -0.011; per cohort: dermoscopic hair $-0.052$ [$-0.066, -0.039$], thyroid $-0.016$ [$-0.021, -0.010$], ovary $-0.011$ [$-0.020, -0.003$], capsule $-0.032$ [$-0.054, -0.009$]), as the linear-Gaussian account predicts.%
