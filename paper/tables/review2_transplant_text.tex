With the artifact outside the ROI masking removes the shortcut entirely---the masked model's reversed, correlated and clean AUROC coincide---whereas with the identical artifact inside the ROI the shortcut survives masking (Supplement~S11). The location interaction is positive in all four cohorts: dermoscopic hair $+0.253$ [$+0.214, +0.293$], thyroid $+0.277$ [$+0.256, +0.298$], ovary $+0.074$ [$+0.044, +0.104$], capsule $+0.497$ [$+0.427, +0.567$] (Table~\ref{tab:law}). \emph{Paste-edge control (pre-registered).} Pasting neutral tissue through the same masks, at the same positions and with the same blending gives a smaller but still positive interaction (dermoscopic hair $+0.204$ [$+0.164, +0.244$], thyroid $+0.145$ [$+0.130, +0.161$], ovary $+0.052$ [$+0.021, +0.084$], capsule $+0.459$ [$+0.383, +0.535$]), 52--92\% of the artifact's. The neutral patch is itself a local, label-correlated pattern that the encoder learns, and masking removes it only outside the ROI. The artifact's content adds significantly to the paste in dermoscopic hair and thyroid (artifact $-$ neutral: dermoscopic hair $+0.049$ [$+0.026, +0.072$], thyroid $+0.132$ [$+0.116, +0.148$], ovary $+0.022$ [$-0.002, +0.048$], capsule $+0.037$ [$-0.017, +0.093$]). By the pre-registered rule the transplant is therefore evidence that \emph{where} a label-correlated pattern sits---not which images carry it---decides what masking removes; it is not evidence about the appearance of any particular artifact, and the in-ROI masking changes it produces (mask $-$ ERM: dermoscopic hair -0.075; thyroid +0.072; ovary -0.010; capsule -0.175) are properties of pasted patterns, not of real artifacts in their natural context. For hair this matters most: a transplanted hair carries the shortcut in its pixels by construction, whereas in the real hair trap the shortcut is carried mostly by correlates of hair (oracle removal of every hair pixel gains only $+0.015$; Sec.~\ref{sec:mech}), so the transplant shows the location effect for pixel-carried hair, not its size for hair in the wild.%
