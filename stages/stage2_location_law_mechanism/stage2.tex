\stagebox{\textbf{In one sentence.} When only the artifact's overlap with the region of interest (ROI) is varied,
ROI masking helps while the artifact lies outside the ROI and stops helping --- in most sweeps it hurts --- once the
artifact overlaps it; the reversal comes from the artifact \emph{surviving} the mask and dominating what the model
sees, not from occlusion, and it recurs in ultrasound, capsule endoscopy and chest radiography with one principled
exception.}
\input{../common/notation.tex}

\section{Where we are}
Stage~1 fixed the protocol (trap environments, frozen foundation-model features, linear heads, grouped splits,
pre-registration, crossed bootstrap) and showed that the pilot's central sweep result replicates. This meeting asks
\emph{why} masking reverses, and \emph{how general} the reversal is. Everything in this report comes from runs
regenerated in \texttt{results/rerun\_2026-09-28/} and every interval uses the corrected (crossed seed $\times$ image)
bootstrap. Relative to earlier drafts, three things are new: (i) the mechanism analyses now carry corrected intervals,
and two lesion-size cells lost significance (Sec.~\ref{sec:changed}); (ii) the same-head counterfactual is tested with a
confidence interval instead of being shown as a figure only; (iii) the controlled sweep is repeated in four further
modalities with two backbones each where possible, and the counts of sweeps that support each part of the law are
generated from the result files.

\section{Question}
\subsection{What we want to know}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q2\alph*},leftmargin=*]\itemsep1pt
\item Is the benefit of masking a function of the artifact's overlap $r$ with the ROI, with a sign change between
$r=0$ and $r=1$? We call a positive \emph{location interaction}
$\big[\mathrm{mask}-\mathrm{ERM}\big]_{r=0}-\big[\mathrm{mask}-\mathrm{ERM}\big]_{r=1}$ on the reversed test the
\emph{location law}.
\item Which mechanism produces the reversal? Three candidates were stated in advance:
\emph{occlusion} (masking removes useful context whatever the artifact does), \emph{lost disease signal} (the
background carries diagnostic information), and \emph{retention/concentration} (the part of the artifact inside the ROI
survives masking and becomes a larger share of the visible pixels, so the head relies on it more).
\item Does the law hold beyond one backbone, one artifact rendering and one modality --- and where does it fail?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why these experiments, and what we rejected}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]\itemsep1pt
\item \emph{Occlusion control instead of arguing from intuition.} If masking harmed because it throws away context, it
would harm even when the artifact carries no label information \citep[cf.][]{fong2019occlusions}. The control keeps the ruler at the same positions but
makes it independent of the label.
\item \emph{Dilation as a dose of retention.} Growing the mask by a margin keeps more of the ruler visible without
changing the lesion. If retention drives the harm, harm should grow with retention when retention is incomplete (50\%
overlap) and stay flat when the ruler is already fully retained (100\% overlap).
\item \emph{Same-head counterfactual instead of saliency maps.} Saliency maps are qualitative and unstable for frozen
encoders. We instead insert and remove the artifact on the \emph{same} test image and read the change in the predicted
probability $|\Delta p|$ of each trained head: a direct, per-image measure of reliance on the artifact.
\item \emph{Lesion size as a natural dose.} The same ruler covers a larger share of a small lesion; if concentration
matters, harm should be largest for small lesions. A fixed ratio threshold was \emph{not} pre-specified, because the
pilot showed none.
\item \emph{Rejected:} measuring reliance through head weights (not comparable between masked and unmasked feature
spaces) and a single ``average'' overlap (it would hide the sign change).
\end{itemize}

\section{Methods}
\paragraph{Controlled sweep} ISIC 2018 pilot cohort (Stage~1), a $65\times19$\,px ruler pasted at a frozen position per
image and overlap $r\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:examples}); DINOv2 ViT-B/14 at 518\,px \citep{oquab2024dinov2}, CLS features,
logistic heads; seeds 42, 123, 456; environments 0.9/0.1 (training, correlated test), 0.1/0.9 (reversed test) and
artifact-free (clean test). The primary outcome is the reversed-test AUROC.
\paragraph{Arms} ERM (unmasked image); \textbf{ROI masking} (pixels outside the lesion mask set to the image mean before
feature extraction); oracle inpainting (the known ruler pixels are replaced by the surrounding skin --- an upper bound
that needs the artifact mask); group-balanced reweighting of the artifact $\times$ label cells; deep feature
reweighting (DFR) on a balanced subset \citep{kirichenko2023dfr}; paired LEACE \citep{belrose2023leace} (the linear concept direction of the artifact is erased using
images with and without it); GroupDRO \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}. All arms share features and seeds; only the input or the head changes.
\paragraph{Occlusion control} The ruler is pasted at the same positions in 50\% of images independently of the label;
AUROC is measured on a 50/50 test.
\paragraph{Mask dilation} The lesion mask is dilated by 0, 10, 25 or 50\,px before masking. \emph{Retention} is the share
of ruler pixels that remain visible; \emph{ruler share} is the ruler's share of all visible pixels.
\paragraph{Same-head counterfactual} For each artifact-bearing image of the reversed test and each trained head, the
image is scored with and without the pasted ruler (the masked head sees the masked version of both); $|\Delta p|$ is the
absolute change in predicted probability. The mask $-$ ERM difference of mean $|\Delta p|$ is bootstrapped with seeds
and images crossed.
\paragraph{Lesion-size strata} Test images are split into tertiles of lesion area; the median ruler-to-lesion area ratio
is reported per tertile.
\paragraph{Replications and other modalities} DINOv2 at 224\,px, the dermatology foundation model DermLIP at 224\,px \citep{yan2025derm1m}, and
a ruler whose colour, tick pattern and size are randomised per image (``stress''). Other modalities, each with its own
ROI masks: thyroid nodules (TN3K \citep{gong2021tn3k}; synthetic caliper; DINOv2 and MedSigLIP-448), ovarian tumours (MMOTU \citep{zhao2022mmotu}; synthetic
caliper; DINOv2), capsule-endoscopy lesions (SEE-AI \citep{seeai2023}; synthetic debris; box ROIs; DINOv2 and MedSigLIP) and pneumothorax
radiographs (NIH \citep{wang2017chestxray8}; synthetic tube; lung masks; RAD-DINO \citep{perezgarcia2025raddino} and DINOv2).
\paragraph{Statistics} Every interval is a 95\% percentile interval from 10,000 crossed bootstrap replicates (seeds
resampled, one Poisson(1) weight per test image shared across seeds and arms). A claim is \emph{supported} if its
pre-stated criterion holds with the interval; ``significant'' below means the 95\% interval excludes zero, not a
multiplicity-adjusted test (the confirmatory family is in Stage~3).

\section{Results}
\subsection{The controlled sweep: the location law}
Masking changes the reversed-test AUROC by \sTwoMaskZero{} when the ruler lies outside the lesion, by
\sTwoMaskFiveZero{} at 50\% overlap and by \sTwoMaskOneZeroZero{} at full overlap (Table~\ref{tab:sweep},
Figure~\ref{fig:dose}); the location interaction is \sTwoInter. Masking therefore \sTwoHarmVerdict. Every other arm
helps at every overlap, and their benefit \emph{grows} with overlap --- the opposite of masking --- because they target
the artifact, not the background. On the correlated test the masked model at full overlap scores \sTwoCorrMaskHundred{}
against \sTwoCorrErmHundred{} for ERM: the masked model exploits the ruler more, which is what the reversed test then
punishes.

property of one encoder, one resolution or one rendering of the ruler.

\subsection{Mechanism}
\paragraph{Occlusion does not explain the harm} With the ruler present but uninformative, masking \sTwoOccVerdict{}
(Table~\ref{tab:occ}); inpainting, which removes only the ruler, changes nothing either. Masking by itself does not cost
the reversed-test AUROC it loses in the sweep.
\input{tables/occlusion.tex}

\paragraph{Retention drives the harm when it is incomplete} At 50\% overlap, dilating the mask from 0 to 25\,px raises
retention from \sTwoRetFiveZeromZero{} to \sTwoRetFiveZeromTwoFive{} and moves the masking effect from
\sTwoDilFiveZeromZero{} to \sTwoDilFiveZeromTwoFive. At 100\% overlap the ruler is already retained
(\sTwoRetOneZeroZeromZero) and the effect stays flat (\sTwoDilOneZeroZeromZero{} without and \sTwoDilOneZeroZeromFiveZero{}
with a 50\,px margin), although dilation \emph{lowers} the ruler's share of visible pixels
(\sTwoShareOneZeroZeromZero{} to \sTwoShareOneZeroZeromFiveZero). Harm therefore tracks whether the ruler is visible,
not how much of the image it occupies once visible (Table~\ref{tab:dil}). The intervals of neighbouring margins overlap;
the pattern, not each step, is the finding.
\input{tables/dilation.tex}

\paragraph{The masked model relies more on the retained artifact} On the same images, inserting the ruler at full
overlap changes the masked head's prediction by \sTwoDpMaskFull{} on average and the ERM head's by \sTwoDpErmFull{}
--- about \sTwoDpRatioFull{} times as much; the paired difference is \sTwoDpDiffFull{} (Table~\ref{tab:cf},
Figure~\ref{fig:cf}). At 0\% overlap the masked head cannot see the ruler (\sTwoDpMaskZero) whereas ERM still reacts to
it (\sTwoDpErmZero). This is the mechanism in one measurement: masking removes the competing evidence, the retained
artifact becomes the most informative remaining feature, and the head leans on it. The counterfactual was saved for the
dermoscopy sweep only; the other sweeps did not store per-image counterfactual scores, so this measurement is not
replicated across modalities.

\caption{Mean same-head sensitivity $|\Delta p|$ to inserting the ruler, masked model (solid) and ERM (dashed), by
overlap (dermoscopy sweep).}\label{fig:cf}\end{figure}

\paragraph{Lesion size} Table~\ref{tab:tert} splits the test set by lesion area. The ruler covers a median
\sTwoRatioLarge{} of a large lesion's area, \sTwoRatioMedium{} of a medium and \sTwoRatioSmall{} of a small one. With
the corrected intervals, the harm at full overlap is significant for small (\sTwoTertSmallOneZeroZero) and medium
lesions (\sTwoTertMediumOneZeroZero) and not for large ones (\sTwoTertLargeOneZeroZero, which \sTwoLargeVerdict); at
50\% overlap it is significant only for small lesions (\sTwoTertSmallFiveZero; medium \sTwoTertMediumFiveZero). What
survives is an ordering --- harm is largest where the artifact is large relative to the lesion --- with wide intervals
because each tertile has few melanomas. No fixed ratio threshold is claimed.

\subsection{Other modalities}
Table~\ref{tab:other} repeats the sweep with synthetic artifacts on real images from four further modalities. Across
all \sTwoNSweeps{} controlled sweeps of this report (four dermoscopy, seven others), the location interaction is
positive with an interval above zero in \sTwoNInterPos{} and negative in \sTwoNInterNeg. Whether the falling benefit
turns into harm at full overlap depends on the setting: masking harms significantly at full overlap in \sTwoNHarmFull{}
sweeps, still helps in \sTwoNHelpFull{} and makes no significant difference in \sTwoNNullFull.
\input{tables/other_sweeps.tex}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Thyroid calipers.} Masking helps strongly with the caliper outside the nodule (DINOv2
\sTwoThyroidcaliperDINOvTwoZero; MedSigLIP \sTwoThyroidcaliperMedSigLIPZero) and the benefit vanishes at full overlap
(\sTwoThyroidcaliperDINOvTwoFull; \sTwoThyroidcaliperMedSigLIPFull): the law holds as a loss of benefit, not as harm.
\item \textbf{Ovarian calipers.} Masking harms at full overlap (\sTwoOvariancaliperDINOvTwoFull) and its benefit
outside the tumour is not significant (\sTwoOvariancaliperDINOvTwoZero): MMOTU tumours fill much of the frame, so little
background is removed.
\item \textbf{Capsule debris.} With DINOv2 the sweep reverses (\sTwoCapsuledebrisDINOvTwoZero{} to
\sTwoCapsuledebrisDINOvTwoFull). With MedSigLIP masking still helps at full overlap
(\sTwoCapsuledebrisMedSigLIPFull) but less than outside (\sTwoCapsuledebrisMedSigLIPZero; interaction
\sTwoCapsuledebrisMedSigLIPInter): the box ROI contains much normal mucosa, so masking removes little of the lesion's
context.
\item \textbf{Chest tube --- the boundary.} With RAD-DINO the ERM model ignores a tube outside the lungs (reversed
\sTwoCxrErmRevZero{} versus clean \sTwoCxrErmCleanZero{} at no overlap), so masking has nothing to remove at $r=0$; at
full overlap ERM collapses (\sTwoCxrErmRevOneZeroZero) and the masked model less so (\sTwoCxrMaskRevOneZeroZero).
The interaction is negative. With DINOv2, which does use the extra-pulmonary tube (reversed \sTwoCxrDinoErmRevZero{}
versus clean \sTwoCxrDinoErmCleanZero), the law holds (\sTwoChesttubeDINOvTwoInter). The law's premise is that the
encoder uses the out-of-ROI artifact; when it does not, masking cannot help outside and the law does not apply.
\end{itemize}

\section{What failed or changed}\label{sec:changed}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Two lesion-size cells lost significance with the corrected bootstrap}: large lesions at full overlap and
medium lesions at 50\% overlap. Earlier drafts described harm as increasing monotonically across all three tertiles at
both overlaps; that statement is withdrawn and replaced by the ordering above. The old and new intervals are listed in
\texttt{results/bootstrap\_correction/sweeps\_umte\_old\_vs\_new.md}.
\item \textbf{Harm at full overlap is not universal.} Earlier drafts said masking ``harms once the artifact overlaps the
ROI''. With corrected intervals, the robust statement is the \emph{interaction} (\sTwoNInterPos{} of \sTwoNSweeps);
harm at full overlap is significant in \sTwoNHarmFull{} of \sTwoNSweeps{} sweeps.
\item \textbf{Chest radiography with RAD-DINO contradicts the law}, as a failure of its premise (above). It was
registered as a confirmation and is reported as a boundary case.
\item \textbf{The counterfactual was not saved for the non-dermoscopy sweeps}, so the mechanism measurement is
dermoscopy-only.
\item \textbf{The ovarian sweep has no significant benefit outside the ROI}, so its crossover rests on the harm side
only.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\textbf{Supported.} The location law in its interaction form (Q2a) holds in \sTwoNInterPos{} of \sTwoNSweeps{}
controlled sweeps across five modalities and four encoders. The mechanism (Q2b) is retention: occlusion alone does not
harm, harm follows the visibility of the artifact under dilation, and the masked head's same-image reliance on an
in-ROI artifact is about \sTwoDpRatioFull{} times that of ERM (\sTwoDpDiffFull). \textbf{Qualified.} Harm at full
overlap is setting-dependent; the lesion-size gradient is an ordering with two non-significant cells. \textbf{Not
supported.} The law for an encoder that ignores the out-of-ROI artifact (chest tube, RAD-DINO). The practical reading
is: masking removes what lies outside the ROI and concentrates the model on what lies inside; whether that helps depends
on where the shortcut sits.

\section{Questions for the supervisor}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item Should the thesis state the law in its interaction form only (robust) and treat harm at full overlap as
setting-dependent, or keep harm as the headline for dermoscopy, where it holds with every encoder?
\item Is one modality (dermoscopy) enough for the counterfactual mechanism, or should the counterfactual be re-run for
thyroid and capsule before submission (about one GPU-hour each)?
\item Is it acceptable to present chest radiography with RAD-DINO as a boundary of the law rather than as a failed
confirmation, given that it was registered as a confirmation?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Next stage}
Stage~3 leaves synthetic artifacts. It asks whether the same crossover appears with \emph{real} artifacts --- hair in
dermoscopy, calipers in thyroid and ovarian ultrasound, debris in capsule endoscopy, devices in chest radiographs ---
where location is defined by real masks and detectors. It also reports the overlap-contrast design that failed (E12),
the detector validity checks and the 16-test confirmatory family with Holm correction.
